# Adaptive practice

Starburst can build deterministic recall quizzes from a learner's knowledge library. A quiz can use explicitly selected concepts, concepts due for review, concepts with mastery at or below 60%, a mix of due and selected concepts, knowledge gaps, or the generated concepts for an owned course or module. Knowledge-gap practice starts with concepts that have no practice history or mastery below 50%, then follows up to three owner-scoped `requires`/`builds_on` links to missing foundations. Learners can select focus concepts for targeted prerequisite practice; otherwise the system picks new and weak concepts. Individual selections are capped at 20; a full course or module can include up to 40 generated concepts.

`POST /api/learning/practice/quizzes` creates an owner-scoped quiz. Course and module modes resolve generated knowledge units from the learner-owned course curriculum and never trust client-supplied unit IDs. The response includes prompts, choices, and two progressive hints, while the answer key remains in the private `adaptive_practice_quizzes` collection. `POST /api/learning/practice/quizzes/{id}/submit` requires one choice per question, then atomically scores the quiz, records `adaptive_practice` evidence, updates mastery and review schedules, and marks the quiz submitted. Rejected or failed submissions do not partially change learner state. A missed definition is returned as remediation after submission.

The `/practice` route offers selected, due, weak, gap, mixed, course, and module modes. Quiz content comes from saved knowledge titles and definitions; no model service is required. Gap questions include a reason such as “Not practiced yet” or “Prerequisite for Integration.” Traversal is cycle-safe, limited to three links, and skips foundations with at least 75% mastery and two successful repetitions. Learners with that mastery history get reverse-recall questions; other concepts use definition recognition. Distractors use definitions or titles from other selected concepts when available and distinct fallback choices when there are too few. Adding clear definitions improves quiz quality. Grammar- or reasoning-specific question types remain future work.
